How should I interpret these benchmarks?
A guide to the numbers. Watch scores rank counties using vote volume, recent swing leverage, closeness and net vote contribution. Historical alignment is a small-sample descriptive percentage—not an election forecast or a calibrated chance of statewide victory. All benchmark votes are hypothetical, and a county leading in early returns does not imply its full vote has been counted.

Methodology, statistical caveats, source provenance and data feed contract

County selection

Each state's ten units are selected mechanically from 2024 presidential returns using a within-state normalized weighted score: 50% ballots cast, 25% absolute 2020–2024 swing multiplied by votes, 15% 2024 two-party competitiveness, and 10% absolute 2024 net vote contribution. These are descriptive priorities, not measured causal importance or a proprietary validated forecast.

2026 winning-pace scenarios

The model benchmark uses the current Original Ervin statewide two-party margin (Ervin's Take) and shifts each county's 2024 presidential Democratic two-party share by the same points. For independent challengers this is an especially rough geographic comparison, not an assumption that all Democratic voters transfer to the independent. The elected winner is determined from complete statewide returns, not the sum of these scenarios.

Turnout ranges: select 55% through 110% of 2024 presidential D+R turnout as an assumed Senate total. The benchmark vote counts are sensitivity calculations and are not voter-file turnout estimates. Attributed live county returns are assessed relative to the model benchmark only when the source marks a county complete; partial reports cannot be treated as representative of the whole county.

Path to victory: once verified statewide candidate votes arrive, any remaining-votes threshold assumes the manually selected turnout scenario and is a conditional arithmetic illustration, not a prediction of how many ballots remain.

Agreement statistic

For comparable 2016, 2020 and 2024 presidential cycles, count times the county plurality party matched its statewide plurality party. The displayed percentage is the Jeffreys-smoothed historical frequency 100 × (matches + 0.5) / (cycles + 1) with the actual count shown. The tiny sample and selection bias prevent interpreting it as the probability of winning a 2026 Senate race conditional on the county; it is not a calibrated chance.

Benchmarks and vote targets

To illustrate a hypothetical statewide 50–50 major-party contest, shift each county's 2024 two-party Democratic presidential share by the points needed to bring the 2024 statewide two-party share to 50%. Scale its 2024 major-party vote by the selected turnout multiplier and apply the resulting share. These thresholds are scenarios, not the 2026 Senate vote projection. Other parties are excluded from this threshold; independents are not interchangeable with Democratic presidential candidates.

Alaska

Alaska uses 2024 state House districts, not counties, and no pre-2024 geographic alignment percentage because redistricting makes the historical district boundaries noncomparable. The 2024 presidential first-choice pattern does not model ranked-choice rounds or the 2026 independent-versus-Republican contest.

Historical sources and election-night verification

2016, 2020 and 2024 county-level presidential results are from Tony McGovern's public compilation (Fox News, Politico and other public returns). This is a secondary dataset, not a separate certified state audit. Current results are accepted only in the site's normalized 2026 Senate county snapshot with an attributed HTTPS source, timestamp, valid county identifier, election/office match, and post-closing reporting time. No simulated votes enter the live table.
