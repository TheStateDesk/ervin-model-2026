# Michigan candidate-strength scientific audit — November 2, 2026

**Not scientifically validated.** El-Sayed's 2026 Democratic primary polling error is observed, but the 20% transfer to a different general election is an editorial judgment with **zero** compatible out-of-sample backtests.

[RCP](https://www.realclearpolling.com/polls/senate/democratic-primary/2026/michigan): +10.2 polling margin (July 8–29) versus +1.0 August 4 result = **9.2-point overestimate**. [270toWin](https://www.270towin.com/2026-senate-polls/michigan): 46.8% versus 39.4% = **7.4-point polling lead**, or **6.4-point overestimate**. These aggregators reuse polls and are not independent replications.

The historical Michigan Senate baseline before candidate context was **D +1.08**. At 20% of RCP's 9.2-point discrepancy (−1.84 points), Ervin's Take is **R +0.76**.

The RCP-based forecast switches winner when more than **11.7%** transfers: the winner is **not robust** to the unvalidated assumption. Primary and general-election electorates and opponents differ. Nine paired Michigan office polling samples show stated candidate-preference differences, not hidden election bias; the samples are correlated rather than independent, making a naïve sign-test p-value unreliable as confirmation. A prior 2018 primary snapshot underestimated El-Sayed, a caution against assuming invariant directional polling bias, although that snapshot is not directly comparable.

No religion-specific, poll-mode, regional demographic, or separate ticket-splitting penalty is supported by a comparable poll-to-election validation sample. These are **not applied**. The 20% candidate-strength sensitivity should remain labeled exploratory within Ervin's Take.

**Prospective falsification:** After certified November 3 results, estimate Senate polling residuals, compare matched gubernatorial/House errors and survey modes, collect additional comparable primary-to-general candidate races and perform true out-of-sample backtesting.
