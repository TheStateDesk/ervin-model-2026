GOVERNORS / POWER IN THE STATES

Four governorships change hands—and governing priorities with them.

Georgia, Iowa, Ohio and Texas elected Democratic governors. Unofficial dated unofficial returns show margins ranging from about seven to eleven points.

THE ERVIN MODEL / DATA DESK • NOVEMBER 4, 2026 • 5 MIN READ • ELECTION RETURNS UNOFFICIAL

The 2026 Democratic victories cannot be summarized by Washington. Voters also elected Democrats to governor offices previously held by Republicans in Georgia, Iowa, Ohio and Texas. The state-executive map changed alongside Congress, opening a different chapter in budget, appointments, regulation and veto negotiations.

Four changes are politically meaningful because governor elections are not simply scaled-down congressional elections. Local candidate reputation, state budgets, and the incumbent record can matter as much as national partisanship. Still, the cross-state pattern in 2026 suggests that the Democratic improvement was broad enough to reach several different levels of government.

Rob Sand's Iowa margin of almost 11 points stands out. Iowa also elected a Democratic senator, Josh Turek, by a narrower margin, suggesting that voters were not simply marking a straight-party ballot in every race. In Georgia, Keisha Lance Bottoms won a statewide executive office while Democratic incumbent Jon Ossoff also secured reelection to the Senate. Those two outcomes were victories for the same party, but they involved different opponents and election dynamics.

Texas gives the state shift national significance

Texas generated perhaps the most conspicuous double result of the night: Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Republican Greg Abbott, while James Talarico won the Senate race. The AP-attributed governor snapshot showed Hinojosa at 52.81% and Abbott at 45.72% with about 94% reported, a lead just over seven points. AP's Texas election account ↗ points to affordability and concerns about corruption as recurring themes.

Texas illustrates why the governor story cannot be folded into Senate arithmetic. A governor controls executive appointments and can sign or veto state legislation. The legislature remains an independent branch, and party control of the state House and Senate cannot be inferred from who won the governor's office.

A new balance between capitals and Washington

When a Republican president faces Democratic majorities in both chambers of Congress, state politics can become more consequential: governors negotiate over federal programs, direct state agencies and organize responses to federal policy changes. But the precise limits depend on laws already enacted and the balance within each state legislature.

Ohio and Iowa underscore the scale of the candidate-level change. Both states voted for Republicans in the 2024 presidential election, yet in 2026 elected Democratic governors and Democratic senators. A national narrative captures part of that movement; local explanations—including candidate selection, the economy and turnout—must still be examined state by state.

The returns were still unofficial on November 4. The durable conclusion is not a precise final margin but the transfer of four governors' offices to Democratic hands. Check AP's updated governor results ↗.
