GOVERNORS / POWER IN THE STATES

Five governors' races flip, with Alaska still uncalled by AP.

Five Republican-held governorships flipped to Democrats. MultiState lists Alaska as a sixth, while AP had not called that race.

GABRIEL ERVIN, PUBLISHER · ANALYSIS • NOVEMBER 4, 2026 • 5 MIN READ • ELECTION RETURNS UNOFFICIAL
Published Nov. 4, 2026. Claims are based on the linked sources. Corrections and editorial standards.
Correction and update · Nov. 4, 2026: An earlier version omitted Nevada and reported only four governorships changing hands. Five Democratic flips have been called. MultiState lists Alaska as a sixth; the AP still reported Alaska uncalled in its Nov. 4 election update. This story distinguishes the two standards.

Democrats gained at least five Republican-held governors' offices in 2026, with Alaska emerging as a possible sixth flip. Called victories in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas changed the balance of power in state capitals alongside the Democratic takeover of Congress.

These offices carry different authority from seats in Congress: governors appoint agency leaders, shape state budgets and sign or veto legislation. But winning a governor's race does not automatically give a party control of a state legislature.

Texas and Nevada shift executive power

In Texas, Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Republican Gov. Greg Abbott while James Talarico won the U.S. Senate race. The Texas Tribune's coverage examines the consequences of the party's statewide sweep. The elections are related but should not be conflated: each office has separate powers, terms and legislative constraints.

Nevada's Aaron Ford defeated Republican Gov. Joe Lombardo, a fifth called flip that was missing from the earlier version of this story. The state's Democratic legislative majorities also make the change particularly consequential for the next legislative session.

Different states, different political coalitions

Rob Sand's win in Iowa coincided with Josh Turek's Democratic Senate victory. Ohio paired Amy Acton's gubernatorial win with Sherrod Brown's election to the Senate. Keisha Lance Bottoms won in Georgia. That cross-office pattern suggests broad Democratic improvement, but detailed turnout and vote-share comparisons require state-specific evidence rather than a single national explanation.

Alaska is harder to classify. Kreiss-Tomkins led in early returns, but under ranked-choice voting a candidate may need a further tabulation. The AP had not called the contest even as MultiState included the state in its six-flip summary. Kansas's governorship was also uncalled in the cited updates.

What happens next

The new governors will face state budgets, negotiations with legislatures and difficult decisions about education, health care, energy and infrastructure. Campaign agendas are proposals, not enacted policy. The consequential measurement in the months ahead will be actual bills, appointments and spending decisions.
