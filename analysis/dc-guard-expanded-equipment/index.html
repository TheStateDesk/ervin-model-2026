National Guard's expanded D.C. equipment plan sharpens the debate over its mission

Patrol cars, military vehicles and boats are authorized, while the limits on soldiers' policing powers remain consequential.

The State Desk · Published Nov. 6, 2026, 2:01 a.m. EST · Updated Nov. 6, 2026, 2:01 a.m. EST

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has authorized an expanded set of vehicles and equipment for National Guard personnel deployed in Washington, D.C., according to separate reporting by the Associated Press, the Washington Post and Fox News. The guidance was signed October 29 and reported November 5. It broadens the equipment available to troops but does not, on its own, confer general arrest powers.

Fox News reported that the authorization covers up to 60 marked patrol cars and up to 20 light towers, along with military vehicles and maritime assets. The Washington Post reported that boats supporting certain security operations must carry a federal law-enforcement officer. These are authorized maximums, not a count of vehicles already operating on the streets.

The dispute concerns both public safety and civilian control. Federal officials say the deployment supports policing and security in the capital. District officials and some residents have objected to military involvement in ordinary neighborhoods, while litigation over the deployment remains pending. The distinction between assisting law enforcement and performing law-enforcement duties is therefore central.

The policy question for the next Congress is whether lawmakers seek closer oversight of mission scope, equipment and deployment costs. No new congressional restriction has been enacted as a result of these reports. The State Desk has not independently inspected the Pentagon memorandum and attributes its description to the news organizations that reported it.
