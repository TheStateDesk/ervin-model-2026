The State Desk / Analysis / SOUTH CAROLINA
SENATE / HEALTH CARE AND AFFORDABILITY

Annie Andrews won South Carolina by making the election about families' bills

The pediatrician's defeat of Darline Graham ended decades without a Democratic Senate victory. Rural health, prescription prices and household expenses are now her governing test.

THE STATE DESK / ORIGINAL ANALYSIS • NOVEMBER 4, 2026 • 3 MIN READ • UNOFFICIAL RETURNS
Published Nov. 4, 2026. Claims are based on the linked sources. Corrections and editorial standards.

South Carolina's Senate election looked almost impossible for Democrats at the beginning of 2026. Donald Trump had carried the state by nearly 18 percentage points two years earlier, and the party had not elected a Democratic U.S. senator since 1998. On November 3, pediatrician Annie Andrews defeated Republican Sen. Darline Graham, a victory independently projected by CBS News and widely confirmed in national reporting.

The contest was unusually shaped by the sudden death of longtime Sen. Lindsey Graham in July and the appointment of his sister, Darline Graham, to finish his term. The Republican incumbent won the special nomination process but faced questions about her national-policy experience. Andrews, a physician and mother, framed the race around children, medical care and the financial pressure on ordinary households.

The State Desk's preserved November 4 vote snapshot showed Andrews at 51.13% and Graham at 46.65%, a 4.48-point Democratic margin with approximately 99% reporting. That snapshot is unofficial and may change. The saved pre-election outside polling index had shown Graham ahead by 0.8 points, making the Democratic improvement 5.28 points—far larger than in the Kansas race.

Andrews's signature priorities come from the years she spent treating children and meeting families in clinical settings. Her platform calls for affordable health insurance, lower prescription-drug prices and protecting rural hospitals. She has opposed cuts to Medicaid and emphasized the practical consequences of losing a hospital or pediatric service in rural communities.

Her economic program also seeks to reduce costs for child care, housing, energy and groceries. A statewide 'Gas, Grits, and Groceries' tour put that message at the center of her campaign. Among the proposals were increasing housing accessibility and giving communities greater power to respond to large data-center developments that may affect land use and energy bills.

The candidate also highlighted Washington ethics and federal spending. Her published platform includes reducing waste, cutting some taxes for South Carolina families, examining congressional perks and paying closer attention to the national debt. Andrews supports abortion rights and stronger gun-violence prevention measures, positions that remain contested in the state's conservative electorate.

Republicans argued that their party offered a clearer path on spending, regulation and public safety. President Trump and national Republican groups spent time and money trying to protect the seat as the race tightened. Reuters estimated that Republican groups spent $27 million defending the seat and Democrats spent $24 million seeking the pickup. The campaign demonstrated a central difficulty for the GOP in 2026: voters worried about the cost of living were willing to consider a Democratic challenger even in a state with a strong Republican presidential majority. Courthouse News Service's state-level reporting found Andrews carried Greenville and York counties, both Republican-leaning in the 2024 presidential race.

Andrews enters the new Senate with an unusual opportunity and an equally large burden. South Carolina voters may approve of her medical experience and affordability agenda without agreeing with every Democratic position. Protecting rural care and advancing household-cost reforms may therefore be more durable first tests than treating the outcome as a broad ideological mandate. Her ability to deliver for families—and to explain the tradeoffs—will matter long after election night.
