We graded our own forecast. The adjustment missed the wave.

In seven Republican-held Senate seats that Democrats flipped, the frozen Ervin correction did substantially worse than the unadjusted polling blend.

GABRIEL ERVIN, PUBLISHER · AI-ASSISTED DATA ANALYSIS • NOV. 4, 2026 • PRELIMINARY RESULTS

Built from the forecast locked Nov. 3, 11:42 a.m. ET and unofficial vote snapshots captured Nov. 4, noon ET. Figures may change as the count proceeds.

The Ervin Model's historical adjustments moved each of these seven Senate contests toward the Republican nominee. The reported results moved in the opposite direction. Across these seven races, the mean absolute margin error was 4.80 points for the frozen raw polling blend and 9.48 points for the adjusted forecast.

The adjustment worked against the direction of the results

In Iowa, the frozen polling average had Josh Turek ahead by just 0.20 point, but the historical adjustment favored Ashley Hinson by 5.96 points. Turek led in the cited returns by 4.11. In Kansas, Adam Hamilton's 3.81-point polling lead became a 4.71-point forecast deficit despite a reported 4.87-point advantage. In Maine, the adjustment flipped a Troy Jackson polling lead into a projected Susan Collins lead.

There is an important difference between missing an election and diagnosing why. The model's Republican-leaning corrections were not supported by outcomes in this selected sample, but that alone does not validate a single countervailing adjustment. Every flipped seat is selected after seeing the election result, a form of selection bias that exaggerates the appearance of a nationwide directional miss.

What we can and cannot conclude

This preliminary audit shows that the adjusted model performed worse than the polling inputs for these seven contests. It does not yet establish how the model performed across all Senate races, governors, or House districts. It also does not evaluate the published win probabilities, which require a Brier score and calibration chart on all forecastable races, not only surprise outcomes.

The raw-polling article on this site uses a different frozen Nov. 2 index. That is why its seven-race comparison produces a slightly different average from the Nov. 3 input blend analyzed here. Both archives are preserved and labeled instead of being silently reconciled after Election Day.

What changes next

For future versions, historical adjustments should be retained only where held-out backtests show that they improve accuracy over a raw polling baseline. Governors must be tested against prior governor elections, and House districts against comparable district evidence. No frozen 2026 forecast will be retrospectively edited. A full-grade dataset should include all relevant races as counts reach near-final or certified status, with winner accuracy, average margin error, and probabilistic calibration reported alongside examples where the model failed.

Our first obligation is to make failure measurable. The unaltered forecast archive and underlying dated snapshots allow readers to reproduce this result independently.
