ECONOMY · MARKETS

The election rally exposed a split inside the technology trade.

Large technology shares and data-center infrastructure stocks reacted differently to the prospect of a new Congress.

November 5, 2026 · By The State Desk

Investors greeted the Democratic congressional sweep with a rally in major U.S. equities on November 4, but not every company associated with artificial intelligence benefited equally. Reuters reported that a group of five large technology companies gained more than $750 billion in combined market value during the session, while some smaller data-center infrastructure businesses fell.

Reuters attributed the divergence partly to a new policy calculation: tighter scrutiny of power-intensive data-center construction might make existing computing infrastructure more valuable while raising uncertainty for highly leveraged expansion projects. This is an interpretation of market pricing, not proof that Congress will enact a specific data-center restriction.

The numbers demand careful handling. A reported $750 billion change is an estimate of market capitalization at a particular point, not cash received by the companies and not a direct measurement of new economic output. A single trading day also cannot establish a durable investment trend.

Independent market coverage in the Guardian described gains in U.S. shares alongside lower bond yields and a weaker dollar as investors reassessed the election and the outlook for federal policy. Reuters separately identified the debt ceiling, fiscal negotiations and artificial-intelligence oversight as possible concerns for investors even before the vote.

The next useful evidence will be concrete: committee assignments, actual legislative proposals, state permitting decisions, electricity pricing and the companies’ own capital-expenditure disclosures. Until those emerge, the market reaction is best understood as a rapid repricing of expectations rather than a settled verdict on economic policy.
