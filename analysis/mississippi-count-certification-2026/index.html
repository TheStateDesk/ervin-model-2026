Mississippi's Senate count is still open. The deadlines matter more than the latest lead.

Uncounted absentee and affidavit ballots, county certification and a limited recount process make an election-night margin provisional.

By The State Desk · Drafted 2026-11-05T13:02:51Z · Updated 2026-11-05T13:02:51Z · Editor approval required

Mississippi's U.S. Senate contest between Democratic challenger Scott Colom and Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith remains unresolved as local election officials verify remaining ballots. The most important distinction is between unofficial returns and certified results: neither a narrow reported lead nor a campaign's estimate establishes a winner.

Secretary of State Michael Watson said on November 4 that 70 of the state's 82 counties had uploaded unofficial returns to the statewide system, according to WLBT's reporting carried by WTVA. The same report said approximately 3,600 absentee-by-mail ballots were still outstanding, with an additional, unquantified number of affidavit ballots awaiting review. That leaves 12 counties not yet reflected in that upload count; it does not mean 12 counties have counted no votes.

County election officials have until November 13 to certify returns. Mississippi Today reported November 4 that the state has no routine automatic or candidate-requested recount procedure for this contest; a court order would be needed. ABC News independently reported the secretary of state's confirmation of that limitation.

The apparent lead has shifted as more votes have been processed. For that reason, this article intentionally does not publish a fresh vote margin from a live tracker without a synchronized timestamp and source snapshot. Mississippi's election process, not an extrapolation from partial returns, will decide the seat.

Democrats have already secured a Senate majority, according to Associated Press and Reuters election reporting. Mississippi could affect its size, not whether the party controls the chamber. The State Desk will treat any later call as a new, separately attributed development.
