Mississippi Senate count awaits county certification

Uncounted absentee and affidavit ballots, county certification and a limited recount process make an election-night margin provisional.

By The State Desk · Nov. 5, 2026

Mississippi's U.S. Senate contest between Democratic challenger Scott Colom and Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith remains unresolved as local election officials verify remaining ballots. No lead is final until Mississippi's counties certify their returns.

Secretary of State Michael Watson said on November 4 that 70 of the state's 82 counties had uploaded unofficial returns to the statewide system, according to WLBT's reporting carried by WTVA. The same report said approximately 3,600 absentee-by-mail ballots were still outstanding, with an additional, unquantified number of affidavit ballots awaiting review. Twelve counties had not yet uploaded returns to the statewide system, though some had counted ballots locally.

County election officials have until November 13 to certify returns. Mississippi Today reported November 4 that the state has no routine automatic or candidate-requested recount procedure for this contest; a court order would be needed. ABC News independently reported the secretary of state's confirmation of that limitation.

The apparent lead has shifted as more votes have been processed. Mississippi's election process, not an extrapolation from partial returns, will decide the seat.

Democrats have already secured a Senate majority, according to Associated Press and Reuters election reporting. Mississippi could affect its size, not whether the party controls the chamber. The State Desk will treat any later call as a new, separately attributed development.
