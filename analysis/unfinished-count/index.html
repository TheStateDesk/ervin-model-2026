THE REMAINING COUNT / HOW TO READ IT

A majority can be decided before every race is.

What uncalled contests, outstanding ballots and provisional leads actually mean after a tumultuous election.

GABRIEL ERVIN, PUBLISHER · AI-ASSISTED ANALYSIS • NOVEMBER 4, 2026 • 5 MIN READ • ELECTION RETURNS UNOFFICIAL
Published Nov. 4, 2026. AI-assisted drafting and analysis; claims are based on the linked sources. Corrections and editorial standards.

By November 4, the broad political verdict was clear: Democrats had gained both chambers of Congress. But the national headline and the detailed count of every race are not identical questions. A majority can be established while some contests remain close enough to require additional ballots, canvasses or recounts.

As election officials continued tabulating, national reporting identified Alaska and Mississippi among the Senate contests worth watching. The list of uncalled races can change at any hour; the live status belongs to a professional decision desk such as the Associated Press, not a static article written on Wednesday afternoon.

Three terms that should not be mixed together

A vote leader is the candidate with more votes in the returns counted so far. A leader can change when outstanding precincts report, mail ballots are added, or errors are corrected. A newsroom call is the AP's conclusion that the trailing candidate no longer has a realistic path to win; it may come before all votes are counted. A certified result is the election authority's legally finalized tabulation, often issued days or weeks afterward.

These categories matter particularly in a close count. A margin of 2,000 votes has a different meaning when nearly all ballots are reported than when a large urban county or several late-arriving batches have not yet been processed.

Why Alaska can require additional patience

Alaska's election procedures introduce complications not visible in a familiar two-candidate plurality chart. Multi-candidate contests and ranked-choice vote redistribution mean the apparent first-preference lead may differ from the final winner. An analyst must understand the rules and the availability of subsequent rounds before describing a candidate as the winner.

Mississippi poses a different kind of reading problem: a narrow reported margin without a newsroom call requires scrutiny of outstanding votes, geographic reporting and the possibility of a recount. At this point neither a partial count nor a projection should be misrepresented as final certification.

Where to find authoritative numbers

The AP publishes direct, continually updated national and state election pages, including race decisions and votes sourced from local election authorities. Open AP's national results ↗ and use the state selector for current counts. The results can be consequential even after the House or Senate majority has already been projected. A majority with only a few seats to spare leaves leaders less room for defections. Close district races also influence local representation and which members chair committees or participate in legislative negotiations.

The responsible rule is simple: preserve the timestamp, say who made the call, and leave an uncalled race labeled uncalled. No forecast, reconstructed precinct estimate or historical model should be colored as a real vote.
