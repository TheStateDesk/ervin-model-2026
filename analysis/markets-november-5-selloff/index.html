Technology rout erases Wall Street's post-election rally

U.S. shares reversed Wednesday's advance, with chipmakers bearing the sharpest losses as investors weighed AI spending and the next Congress.

By The State Desk · Published Nov. 5, 2026, 4:14 p.m. ET · Updated Nov. 5, 2026, 4:14 p.m. ET · Analysis of attributed market reporting

Wall Street's election-week rally gave way to a broad selloff Thursday, led by the semiconductor companies that had been among the market's most closely watched growth stocks. A Yahoo Finance market report updated at 2:37 p.m. Eastern showed the Dow Jones Industrial Average down about 3%, the S&P 500 down 4% and the Nasdaq Composite down 5.2%. Those figures are an intraday snapshot, not verified closing prices.

The technology losses were more severe. Yahoo Finance reported the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down about 10% as shares of Nvidia, Micron and other chip companies weakened. That contrasts with Wednesday's broad post-election advance: Charles Schwab's market commentary described gains across nine of the S&P 500's eleven sectors, led by consumer discretionary shares. The contrast suggests investors are separating expectations for consumer-facing businesses from concern over the cost and potential regulation of artificial-intelligence infrastructure.

There is more than one explanation for the reversal. Reuters' November 5 global-markets briefing connected the earlier rally to hopes of political gridlock, lower energy prices and a possible diplomatic opening in the Iran war, while also noting pressure on Asian technology shares. Those developments do not establish that the election itself caused Thursday's decline. Earnings, AI valuations, oil prices and the prospect of new federal scrutiny all complicate the picture.

What bond and energy markets are signaling

Bond markets provided another part of the story. MarketWatch reported the 10-year Treasury yield around 4.880% Thursday, down from a recent peak of 5.310% roughly a month earlier. The 0.430-percentage-point difference equals 43 basis points, an independently calculated measure of the change in borrowing-market expectations. Lower yields can reduce financing costs, but they may also reflect concerns about growth or demand for new investment.

Oil prices have moved sharply as investors reconsider the course of the Iran conflict. Reuters described Brent crude trading below $100 earlier Thursday; Yahoo Finance later reported prices near $97 a barrel. These readings come from different observation times and should not be treated as a continuous price series. Energy costs remain important to inflation, consumer spending and the Federal Reserve's policy choices.

Friday's October employment report will offer another test of the economy's strength. Schwab's November 5 preview cited market expectations of about 65,000 additional payroll jobs, compared with 29,000 in September. These are forecasts, not reported October results.

Thursday's trading demonstrates how quickly investors can reconsider a political narrative. The next Congress will influence oversight and fiscal debates, but corporate earnings, energy supply, interest rates and labor-market data will also shape what happens next. The figures in this article reflect cited intraday reporting and should be read with their timestamps.
