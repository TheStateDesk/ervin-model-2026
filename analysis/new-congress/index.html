WASHINGTON / THE NEW CONGRESS

What divided government looks like now

The new Congress will be controlled by Democrats, while the White House remains Republican. The shift changes the math of legislation and oversight.

THE ERVIN MODEL / DATA DESK • NOVEMBER 4, 2026 • 3 MIN READ

Democrats' House and Senate majorities are projected, not yet a final roster of certified winners. The House requires 218 votes for a majority when all 435 seats are filled; a 100-member Senate ordinarily requires 51 seats for control.

The political consequence is a divided federal government beginning in January 2027: Democrats can set the congressional agenda and direct committee oversight, but legislation still faces a Republican president's veto and the Senate's procedural requirements.

According to the Associated Press, the incoming Democratic leadership has discussed affordability, health programs and investigations as priorities. The extent to which these promises become law will depend on negotiations, vetoes, and the size and cohesion of the final majorities.
