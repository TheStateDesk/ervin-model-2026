HEALTH POLICY · THE NEXT AGENDA

Health care is the first test of what the new majority can deliver.

Democrats want to reverse coverage cuts and restore marketplace aid, but a presidential veto remains an obstacle.

November 5, 2026 · By The State Desk

Health care is moving from campaign promise to legislative test after Democrats won control of the House and Senate. The Washington Post reported on November 4 that incoming Democratic leaders intend to seek restoration of expired Affordable Care Act marketplace subsidies and reversal of Medicaid reductions in the 2025 tax law.

The proposals are distinct. Marketplace subsidies affect the net premiums paid by eligible people who buy coverage through Affordable Care Act exchanges. Medicaid is a federal-state coverage program whose financing and eligibility rules have different legal and budget consequences. Treating both as a single policy would conceal the choices facing lawmakers and state administrators.

According to the Washington Post’s reporting, the subsidy expiration is expected to increase costs for some households, while a projected average rise of about 15 percent in exchange premiums next year adds to affordability concerns. That forecast is not a statement that every enrollee will face a 15 percent increase: actual changes depend on location, plan selection, income and available subsidies.

Democratic control of Congress creates an opportunity to hold hearings, move bills through committees and negotiate spending terms. It does not guarantee enactment. President Trump retains the veto, and ordinary contested Senate legislation faces the cloture threshold. The next Congress could pass a proposal that fails to become law, or it could negotiate a narrower compromise with the White House.

The policy disagreement is substantial. Supporters of restoring subsidies argue that premium assistance protects coverage and household budgets. Opponents can raise the long-term federal cost, insurer incentives and whether temporary aid should be extended without broader reform. Similar trade-offs surround Medicaid eligibility rules, federal matching funds and state budget obligations.

Three benchmarks will distinguish a governing plan from a message: whether the majority releases bill text, whether it provides a credible cost estimate, and whether it identifies a path through the Senate and the White House. A roll-call vote may reveal priorities; only an enacted measure changes the law.
