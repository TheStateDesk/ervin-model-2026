October hiring strengthens, but pay is still chasing prices

A stronger jobs report gives Washington a firmer employment picture while leaving household purchasing power unresolved.

THE STATE DESK · Published 2026-11-06T13:58:54-05:00 · Updated 2026-11-06T13:58:54-05:00

The American labor market delivered a stronger-than-expected October report on Friday, complicating the economic picture just days after the midterm elections. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that employers added 165,000 jobs and that unemployment stood at 4.1 percent. Those figures describe continued hiring, not an economy free of strain.

The agency's November 6 release identifies professional and business services, health care, and transportation and warehousing among the sectors adding workers. The Financial Times and Guardian separately reported that hiring exceeded economists' expectations, while cautioning that inflation and borrowing costs remain central concerns.

The contrast between employment and purchasing power is especially important. Average hourly earnings rose 3.1 percent over the year, according to coverage of the report, while September consumer-price inflation was reported at 3.7 percent. Subtracting the two published annual rates gives a 0.6-percentage-point shortfall; that is a comparison of nominal wage and price growth over differently ending reference periods, not a precise real-wage calculation for October.

The report also revised August and September payroll estimates upward by a combined 84,000, according to the Financial Times and other contemporaneous coverage. Revisions matter because a single month's headline can overstate a turning point. The next question for Congress and the Federal Reserve is whether the economy can preserve employment gains while reducing pressure on household budgets.
