Trump names Stephen Miller chief of staff as Washington prepares for divided government

The personnel change takes effect Monday, shifting the White House's management as Democrats prepare to lead Congress.

Prepared November 6, 2026, 8:04 p.m. EST · AI-assisted editorial proposal · No original interviews conducted

President Donald Trump said Friday that Stephen Miller will become White House chief of staff on Monday, November 9, replacing Susie Wiles in the administration's most senior staff position. The president announced the decision on Truth Social; CBS News reported the announcement at 2 p.m. Eastern on November 6. Wiles is expected to move to Trump's political operation.

The appointment is a significant organizational decision immediately after the midterm elections delivered control of both chambers of Congress to Democrats. Miller has served as deputy chief of staff and is closely associated with the president's immigration and executive-branch policies. A chief of staff coordinates access to the president, staffing and internal priorities, but the position does not itself confer new statutory authority.

The Associated Press and Reuters independently reported the change on Friday. NPR also confirmed the replacement and the planned Monday start date. Trump described Miller as a trusted adviser, while Democratic lawmakers criticized the choice. Those reactions point toward sharp oversight disputes next year, though they do not establish how the new chief of staff will handle negotiations or individual legislation.

The transition creates an immediate practical test: how the White House will manage requests for testimony and documents from committees led by the incoming majority, while continuing the administration's policy program. Congress's ability to pass legislation will remain constrained by presidential veto power and Senate rules. The appointment is confirmed; any prediction of specific executive orders or investigations remains prospective.
