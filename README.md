## Dashboard
An experimental October 23, 2026 Senate election research snapshot. Includes a 50-state interactive cartogram, 17 watched Senate races, five polling-source views (RealClearPolling, Decision Desk HQ, 270toWin, Wikipedia average-of-aggregators, Silver Bulletin), adjustable historical bias, historical evidence audit, 80%/90% heuristic scenarios, and downloadable CSV files.

## Historical inputs: at least three per race
The dataset now contains **51 election comparisons across 17 races, 3 for every race**. They are *past-election calibration inputs*, not claims of three independent 2026 polling aggregates per race.

- Prioritize **2018, 2020, 2022 and 2024**. Observations from before 2018 are excluded.
- Prefer **U.S. Senate races**; use statewide **presidential elections** when fewer Senate comparisons exist. Each observation is labeled Senate or PRES in the per-race chart.
- Poll values are the non-Republican candidate's margin over the Republican candidate, so negative values are Republican leads.
- Polling error = forecast poll margin **minus** actual vote margin (positive = Republican strength understated by polling).
- Weight historical Senate observations by **1.0** and presidential observations by **0.65**. Apply recency weights: **2018: 0.70; 2020: 0.80; 2022: 0.90; 2024: 1.00**. The calibration shift is the weighted mean of the historical polling error.
- A Senate and presidential election from the same year are **correlated observations**, not independent calibration cycles. Three historical comparisons do not ensure three independent years.
- **Nebraska — Dan Osborn:** Candidate-specific 2024 Senate average **Osborn −1.6** vs. actual **Osborn −6.67**, a **+5.07-point Republican polling understatement**, applied against his 2026 margin. The Republican opponent changes from Deb Fischer to Pete Ricketts. The 2020 and 2024 presidential comparisons remain audit context only.
- **South Dakota — Brian Bengs:** Candidate-specific 2022 Senate poll **Bengs −25** vs. actual **Bengs −43.5**, a **+18.50-point Republican polling understatement**, applied as a **high-uncertainty single-observation scenario**. The 2026 contest has a new Republican opponent and Bengs runs as an independent rather than a Democrat. The 2020 Senate and 2024 presidential comparisons remain audit context only.
- **Montana — Seth Bodnar:** No suitable repeat-candidate calibration; historical partisan D/R correction **not applied** to this 2026 independent matchup.
- The **small-sample shrinkage** switch retains a stronger reduction (25% of raw candidate-specific error) for Nebraska and South Dakota. The 80%/90% uncertainty scenarios inflate uncertainty, especially in South Dakota. None of these adjustments represents a validated 2026 win probability.
- Some safely Republican states have only **single polls** or **partisan-sponsored surveys**. Such inputs are labeled; grades and scenario bands are heuristic, not validated election probabilities.

### Audit files
- [Historical polling audit (CSV)](historical_error_audit.csv): 51 poll margins, election results, errors, election types, weights, notes, and links.
- [2026 polling sources (CSV)](source_snapshot.csv): 85 source-view entries, including explicit proxy flags.
- [Automated validation script](scripts/validate_data.py): checks minimum 3 inputs per race, 2018–24 windows, office types, numeric error consistency, source links, and independent-matchup exclusions.

The historical polling audit is *not* refreshed by the live-polling checker. Extending this methodology to additional election years requires revisiting office comparability and recency weights.

## Freshness
The website contains a dated **Oct 23, 2026** research snapshot. Publication alone does **not** guarantee newly published polls are ingested. A GitHub Actions polling checker runs approximately every six hours and accepts only matching current-year general-election aggregate tables that satisfy its candidate/source/date guards. Its public [refresh audit](refresh_log.json) distinguishes checked sources, accepted updates, and errors. The website does not claim that a successful check means every publisher has a new average.

## Pilot blend backtest, October 23, 2026
On the initial October 23 pilot, the **Ervin’s Take** blending starting shares were **RCP 35%, DDHQ 26%, 270toWin 19%, and Silver Bulletin 20%**. They are **provisional** and deliberately remain close to the previous 40/25/15/20 setup. See [the row-level benchmark](blend_backtest_2024.csv) for historical inputs and sources.

The pilot compares **2024 Ohio, Texas, Florida and Montana Senate** final aggregate margins for the first three publishers against election margins. To avoid direct look-ahead leakage, the **Original Ervin correction for each tested state is reconstructed exclusively from its pre-2024 historical records** in the project. Backtest average absolute errors, in margin points: **RCP 3.95, DDHQ 3.77, 270toWin 4.12** (four races each). The results are sensitive to the Montana 2020-derived error; these sample sizes cannot support aggressive model tuning. Since comparable Silver Bulletin *Senate polling-average* history was not verified for these four, **Silver has no tested accuracy number and initially retained a 20% provisional weight**, not a claimed measured ranking. DDHQ's final polling average must not be conflated with its probability forecast. No overlapping constituent poll is treated as an additional observation. This is a pilot retrospective diagnostic, not out-of-sample proof of predictive accuracy. Actual 2026 forecasts still apply **Ervin +** after the composite, not the tested Original Ervin mode; those are distinct methodology stages.

## Ervin + historical calibration (October 23, 2026)
**Ervin +**, now an optional adjustment (the **Original Ervin** correction is the default), uses [88 reconstructed 2016–2024 Senate/presidential historical polling-error events](research/calibration_events_2016_2024.json) for the 17 tracked states. It partially pools each state's historical error toward common regional and national patterns, discounts older and thinner evidence, and guards the independent/returning-candidate races separately. Reconstructed averages do not imply 88 independent election cycles. Original Ervin and Shrink small samples preserve the previous limited historical-calibration method.

[Full methodology, coverage and validation](research/CALIBRATION_2016_2024_REPORT.md) · [Calibration source code](scripts/calibration_engine.js) · [Past-only backtest command](scripts/backtest_calibration.js) · [Evidence synchronization and finite-forecast validator](scripts/validate_calibration.js).

The 63-event past-only diagnostic found raw mean absolute error **4.06 points** vs pooled correction **3.28 points**. The 2018/2022 subsets became worse, the 2020/2024 subsets improved. This is not a proof of true out-of-sample superiority or calibrated 80/90% uncertainty coverage. Silver/DDHQ/270toWin/RCP blend weights remain provisional and unchanged. Governor elections were not added to the historical calibration; they need a separate reliably matched source archive and office-effects validation.

At the user's request, Silver Bulletin was reduced from 20% to 10% because a matched, reliable historical Senate-polling-average backlog has not been established for that product. The freed ten percentage points were reallocated approximately pro rata among RCP, DDHQ and 270toWin (rounded to whole percentage points). These are **user-directed, provisional starting shares**, not fitted accuracy probabilities. The website excludes flagged proxies and duplicate publisher identities (not merely identical citation URLs), adjusts for freshness, renormalizes the actually available publishers within each state, and then applies Ervin + calibration. Different publisher averages often appear on the same Wikipedia page; dropping them solely for sharing the citation would incorrectly collapse the blend to one source. Earlier archived source-ranking reports record the then-current shares for historical context.

## Automatic 83-county clerk website checking
The [county watcher workflow](.github/workflows/watch-michigan-clerks.yml) checks the official county election website directory and selected verified local result portals. It runs on GitHub's best-effort scheduler (every six hours normally, up to every 15 minutes during the configured election-night interval), compares link/page changes, and publishes the [83-county source status](michigan/election-night/clerk_watch.json) and [change notices](michigan/election-night/clerk_watch_events.json). The Election Night page includes a searchable table of the checks. A website responding, a report link appearing, or an HTML page changing **does not mean real precinct votes have been captured**. Most counties still require independently verified report-to-CSV intake; the separate experimental Kent fast lane can publish only when its exact post-close source checks succeed. Source pages with login restrictions or anti-bot blocks may not be machine-readable. See the [operator runbook](research/ELECTION_NIGHT_RUNBOOK_2026.md).

## Shared design and county-informed precinct outlook (October 26, 2026)
The national forecast, Michigan forecast and Election Night pages now share a boxed-**E** masthead system, mobile menu and Back to Top action. Election Night county selections are shareable via `?county=Oakland`. Its 2026 precinct boundary map defaults to a **county-informed outlook**, with separate choices for **actual uniquely matched precinct returns** or the **2024 county U.S. Senate baseline**. This new layer:
- Recreates the existing Michigan dashboard's **Original Ervin** 2026 modeled county shares (2024 Senate 55%, 2022 governor 25%, 2024 president 20%, calibrated to the current Michigan statewide Ervin's Take poll scenario).
- Colors every *current 2026 precinct polygon in a county* using that county's same two-party modeled share; **no individual precinct vote counts, turnout, or differentiated precinct winners are inferred** without matched historical precinct returns.
- Once a verified county-wide 2026 clerk tally arrives, displays that county's *published county-wide* share instead of its modeled share, again applied uniformly as a **county proxy**, not invented precinct results.
- Keeps the original uniquely matched 2026 actual precinct returns layer completely separate. An explicit model badge, layered legend, source context, and separate county-detail model text prevent confusion.
This is an approximate geographic rendering, **not a genuine precinct-level forecast**. A true precinct model would require historical precinct returns matched to the 2026 boundary layer and normalized for changes in precinct geography and absentee ballot reporting.

## November 1, 2026: Election-night reporting and map release

The national [Election Center](https://geervin.github.io/ervin-model-2026/elections/overview/) now has a separate election-night reporting desk with office filters (Senate, Governor, House), candidate totals, precinct-reporting progress where provided, source timestamps, freshness status, and attributed official-source results. It refreshes an open page at most once per minute. The underlying Open America results feed covers **22 supported state adapters** and is **not** an all-50-state results vendor. Unsupported races stay unreported rather than displaying synthetic zeros.

Each [race dossier](https://geervin.github.io/ervin-model-2026/elections/) has an original **Ervin margin track**: the pre-election historical-error-adjusted Senate polling margin (or explicitly unadjusted governor publisher average) and the *current* reported-vote D–R margin, shown as separate markers. This is deliberately **not a live estimate of the ultimate margin**: that requires representative geographic reporting/expected outstanding vote methodology that this site does not have. House district polling margins are not manufactured from qualitative ratings. Do not interpret either indicator as an election call.

National geography layers:
- State outlines/polling research remain on the Election Center map.
- A new all-county outline layer renders Census-derived county boundaries with native projection; county-click opens the relevant state's election directory. No fictitious 2026 county fills.
- Every single-state dossier has clickable geographic counties (external us-atlas fallback, locally hosted Michigan 83-county polygons) plus historical color options, county find and expandable viewport. The shape is a **state context map**, not a congressional district boundary for House dossiers.
- The Michigan Senate race dossier optionally colors supported 2026 counties from the same locally published, documented manual/automatic Michigan clerk result snapshots. Both are validated for the 2026 contest, signed timestamps after each county's local poll close, attributed HTTPS sources, bounded safe vote integers, and district/cohort identity. Parallel source snapshots are *deduplicated per county*, not summed. Unreported counties are neutral.

Michigan's [election-night dashboard](https://geervin.github.io/ervin-model-2026/michigan/election-night/) also includes a two-marker original margin track and an expandable official [Michigan Secretary of State voter participation dashboard](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/elections/election-results-and-data/voter-participation-dashboard). That state participation report uses its Qualified Voter File and updates **daily**, beginning 45 days before the election; Election Day voter history is entered by clerks later. It is **not** continuous election-night turnout, and its numbers are not to be added to candidate votes. **Votes counted ≠ voter turnout percentage ≠ percentage of precincts reporting.** The site does not claim a nationwide live turnout pipeline.

**Operations and reliability:** Pages built on GitHub Pages are static. The browser polls published JSON files and cannot force a state to publish. Michigan clerk-site health monitoring does not automatically ingest every clerk's tabulation. On the county page, locally published precinct and clerk feeds are checked every 30 seconds after polls close; the nationwide state results API is checked every 60 seconds. Source outage, unmatched race, missing timestamp, stale data, and unreported counties are explicitly labeled. A reported precinct percentage is never rewritten as percentage of expected votes outstanding. Source: [Open America results API contract](https://openamerica.io/elections/api/).

**Smoke checks completed when release was authored:** the changed election-night JavaScript assets parse successfully; every directly accessed UI id in the new scripts appears in the corresponding HTML documents. This is not a substitute for live browser/Election Day end-to-end tests, provider load tests or confirmation of exact live-feed coverage on November 3.


## November 1, 2026: Map reliability, individual districts, and county research revision

**Local geography, no runtime external county atlas.** The race dossiers and House explorer use \`scripts/geo-engine.js\`, with no D3/TopoJSON dependency for per-state rendering. The browser reads locally hosted \`geo/counties/{state}.json\` or \`geo/districts/{state}.json\` where available, otherwise \`geo/packs/p01.json\` through \`p10.json\` (five states per pack). Alaska uses its separate verified local county and House files; Texas has its own 254-county file. Coverage validated: **50 states, 3,143 county-equivalent shapes, 438 historic House geography shapes, and all 435 indexed House seats matched**. The 2022 Census/TIGER 118th-Congress boundaries are map references, **not certified 2026 House district boundaries**, particularly in redistricted states. Source: [Civic Interconnect](https://github.com/civic-interconnect/civic-data-boundaries-us) and [118th district source](https://github.com/civic-interconnect/civic-data-boundaries-us-cd118); state geometry is simplified, so it should not be used for legal boundary or precinct assignments.

**House map-first design.** [House explorer](https://geervin.github.io/ervin-model-2026/house/#districtExplorer) contains clickable district shapes with hover/title, keyboard Enter/Space selection, direct links to election dossiers, a district picker and a state selector. The full 435-district list is preserved under a collapsed detail instead of being the primary browsing surface. The [Election Center](https://geervin.github.io/ervin-model-2026/elections/overview/) can drill from a state to its locally drawn House district shapes and county lines.

**All-county research.** \`geo/county-history.json\` has 3,160 sourced 2024 and (where published) 2020 presidential county margin records, transcribed from [tonmcg's state/county presidential results archive](https://github.com/tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results_08-24). The Senate and governor dossier's conditional 2026 county scenario applies the race's known statewide adjusted margin (for Senate) or published governor polling margin to historical county margins, retaining a small 2020–2024 relative-county trend and recentering on a statewide turnout-weighted mean. This is a **research scenario, not independently measured 2026 county polling or official election-night results**. Counties and candidate-specific congressional districts do not share identical electorates; because there is no audited House district allocation model, House pages show the geographic district outline and statewide historical county context but **do not invent numerical 2026 district/county expectations**. The county research table supports search, sort, and CSV export; every 2026 scenario is labelled conditional, and absent history stays blank.

**Poll expansions.** \`house/research-polls.json\` indexes the latest D–R public individual survey for **110 House districts**, including generic party matchups and partisan-sponsored pollsters. These are **not race averages**, even where the source lists other surveys. Listings are attributed to [People's Poll House directory](https://peoplespoll.com/house), which cites polling compilations from the New York Times under CC BY 4.0; see [license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and [People's Poll methodology](https://peoplespoll.com/methods). The source currently catalogues 356 surveys in 119 districts, but not every race has a comparable D–R matchup. \`governors/recent_verified_polls.json\` adds 42 dated individual survey listings across 19 states, sourced from RealClearPolling's governor listings. Governor and Senate publisher averages are never counted as independent individual polls; Senate dossier list length was extended to 40 where available.

**Mobile correction.** Small-screen site header is no longer sticky, the floating Back to Top button is disabled at mobile breakpoints, and county map/forecast cards have increased height and safe wrapping. Unit/static checks cover JS syntax and DOM element references; state GeoJSON structure and all indexed House district matches were checked. True remote/browser performance, automatic publication via GitHub Pages, accessibility with assistive technology, and election-night API readiness require further live tests. The maps never simulate votes while the official source is unavailable.


**Release integrity checks (November 1).** `node scripts/validate-election-site.mjs` validates 50 state boundary packs, the 435 House-seat IDs against voting-district shapes (excluding non-voting `ZZ` placeholders), historical county baseline availability, House and governor polling structure, JavaScript parseability, and referenced local CSS/JS assets. `.github/workflows/website-integrity.yml` is configured to run the same test on pushes and pull requests involving election sources. The Michigan and Texas local map assets were independently checked for finite polygon coordinates, and mobile ruler labels were adjusted to avoid overlapping data cards. A successful commit is not itself confirmation that all visitors see the new GitHub Pages deployment immediately; a full live Safari end-to-end test remains necessary.
